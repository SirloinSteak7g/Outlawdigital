Tipsy Telephone

A physical wedding guestbook, powered by a fully automated digital platform.

Tipsy Telephone started with a simple idea: let wedding guests pick up a real telephone and leave a message for the couple. Behind that simple interaction, we built the technology that handles everything that happens next.

The result is an end-to-end system that takes recordings from the physical phone, processes and organises them, and delivers them to each couple through their own private digital answering machine.

Guests don’t need an app, an account or any instructions beyond picking up the phone.

Behind the scenes, the platform does the work.

* Automated recording processing – messages captured by the telephone are transferred into the platform and associated with the correct wedding.
* Private customer galleries – every couple receives their own password-protected digital answering machine where all of their messages live.
* Interactive retro audio player – rather than presenting couples with a folder of MP3 files, the recordings are turned into an experience designed around the original telephone concept.
* Listen, skip, replay and download – couples can work through their messages just like an answering machine and keep the original recordings permanently.
* Individual message sharing – a single recording can be shared back with the person who left it, making thank-yous and particularly memorable messages easy to send.
* Flexible message delivery – couples can receive the complete collection at once or have recordings revealed gradually, extending the experience beyond the wedding day.
* Automated customer communication – links, access details and recording delivery can be handled by the system rather than manually preparing each wedding.
* Venue management system – partner venues can run their own Tipsy Telephone from a simple dashboard. Staff enter the couple’s details, select how they want their recordings delivered, and the platform takes care of the rest.
* Reusable hardware, individual experiences – the same physical telephone can move from wedding to wedding while the software creates a completely separate, secure experience for every couple.

The interesting bit

We didn’t just build the booking website. We built the product behind the business.

The physical phone is only the visible part of Tipsy Telephone. The software turns what could have been a labour-intensive rental service into a repeatable system: capture the recordings, identify the wedding, organise the files, create the customer experience and deliver everything automatically.

That means the technology isn’t simply supporting the business. It’s what allows the business model to work at scale.

For Outlaw Digital, I’d actually make that last idea the centre of the case study. It demonstrates something much stronger than “we make websites”: Outlaw can take a business idea and build the software infrastructure required to operate it.
